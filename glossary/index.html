A

A/B test
Showing two versions of a page, ad or email to similar audiences to see which performs better. Change one thing at a time, or you will not know what made the difference.
Accessibility
Designing so people with disabilities can use what you make: readable contrast, keyboard access, captions, clear language. It also makes things better for everyone else.
AEO
Answer engine optimisation. Making your content easy for AI assistants and answer boxes to find, trust and quote, so the answer people hear names you.
Art direction
Deciding how images should look and feel, from composition to light to casting, so every photo and video belongs to the same brand.
Attribution
Working out which channels and touchpoints led to a sale. Every model is a simplification; choose one, understand its bias, and stick with it.
Audit
A structured review of what you already have: channels, assets, results, competitors. The starting point of any strategy worth trusting.

B

Bounce rate
The share of visitors who leave after one page without doing anything. Useful as a signal, misleading as a goal on its own.
Brand guidelines
The rulebook for applying a brand: logo, type, colour, imagery, voice and templates. Good guidelines are used daily, not filed.
Brand platform
The foundation document of a brand: purpose, positioning, personality, promise and proof. Everything visible is built on it.
Brief
A short document that defines a project: goal, audience, constraints, budget and deadline. The best briefs fit on one page.

C

Call to action
The one thing you ask someone to do next, like "Book a table" or "Start a trial". One clear call beats three competing ones.
Conversion rate
The share of visitors who complete the action you want, such as buying or booking. The most honest number on most websites.
Copywriting
Writing words that make people understand and act. In our view, the most underrated part of design.
CPA
Cost per acquisition. What you pay in media to win one customer or sale. More useful than cost per click.
CPM
Cost per mille: the price of a thousand ad impressions. Common in media buying for outdoor, video and social.

D

Design system
A shared library of components, rules and tokens that lets teams build consistent interfaces quickly.
Design token
A named value, like a colour or spacing step, stored once and used everywhere, so a brand change happens in one place.

E

E-commerce
Selling online. A good store is less about the shop window and more about product pages, checkout and delivery.
Engagement rate
Interactions divided by reach on social media. Worth watching, but never a substitute for business results.

F

First-party data
Information you collect directly from your customers with their consent. Increasingly the most valuable data a brand owns.
Funnel
A model of the steps from first hearing about you to buying. Real people rarely move in a straight line, but the model helps planning.

G

Grid
The invisible structure of columns and spacing behind a layout. It is why good pages feel calm.

H

Hierarchy
The order in which a reader notices things on a page. Set by size, weight, contrast and position.

I

Identity
The visible system of a brand: logo, type, colour, imagery and graphic devices, designed to work together.
Information architecture
How content is organised and labelled so people can find what they need. The skeleton of a website.

K

KPI
Key performance indicator. The few numbers that tell you whether a plan is working. If everything is a KPI, nothing is.

L

Landing page
A page built for one audience and one action, usually the destination of an ad or campaign.
Local SEO
Search optimisation for nearby customers: business listings, reviews, location pages and maps.
Logo
The mark that signs a brand. Important, but only one part of an identity.

M

Media buying
Negotiating and booking ad placements across outdoor, radio, print and digital at the right price and time.
Media plan
The schedule of where, when and how much you will spend on advertising, and why.
Mockup
A realistic preview of a design in use, like a logo on a bag or a site on a phone. A tool for decisions, not a deliverable.
Motion design
Animation applied to brands and interfaces: logo animations, explainers and the transitions that guide attention.

P

Positioning
The specific place you want to hold in your customers' minds compared with the alternatives. Choosing it means choosing what you are not.
Prototype
A working draft of a product or flow that people can click through, used to test ideas before building them.

R

Responsive design
Layouts that adapt to every screen size, from phone to wide desktop.
Retargeting
Showing ads to people who have already visited your site or app. Effective in moderation, irritating in excess.
ROAS
Return on ad spend: revenue divided by advertising cost. A ROAS of 4 means four in sales for every one spent.

S

SEO
Search engine optimisation. Improving your site's technical health, content and reputation so it ranks for the searches that matter.
Sitemap
A map of every page on a website, used both for planning and to help search engines crawl the site.
Social media strategy
The plan for which platforms you use, what you publish, how often and why. Consistency beats volume.
Structured data
Code that labels content for machines: prices, reviews, opening hours, events. It helps search engines and assistants understand your pages.

T

Tone of voice
How a brand sounds in writing and speech. Defined by a few clear rules and plenty of examples.
Typography
The choice and arrangement of type. Most of what people read on your brand is typography, so it carries most of the brand.

U

UI
User interface: the screens, controls and visual details people interact with.
UX
User experience: how it feels to use a product or site, from finding things to finishing a task.

W

Wireframe
A simple layout sketch showing structure and content priority before visual design begins.
Wordmark
A logo made of the brand name set in distinctive lettering. Ours turns the x in Praxis into a star.
